Vehicle-to-grid, from the ECU to the back end.

iso15118 is the ISO 15118 protocol suite in pure Rust: a schema-informed EXI codec generated from the official schemas, both message sets, the SLAC link-setup and SECC discovery state machines, and sans-I/O session engines for the vehicle and the charging station alike. No sockets, no clock, no async runtime — those are yours.

Why another implementation

ISO 15118 is what your car and a DC fast charger speak while deciding whether to move 350 kW between them. The implementations that exist are C, C++ or Python, and none of them runs unchanged on both a charge-point back end and a bare-metal microcontroller. This one is built on four decisions that make that possible.

01

Sans-I/O

Nothing opens a socket or reads a clock. Bytes and a timestamp in; bytes, events and deadlines out. The pattern behind quinn-proto and rustls — and the reason a whole DC charging session runs as a unit test in microseconds, with time as a variable.

02

no_std + alloc

The protocol core builds for thumbv7em-none-eabihf. Feature flags are additive and every one gates real code, so a DC-only embedded vehicle controller compiles the DC message set and nothing else — grammar tables included.

03

A wire format someone else agrees with

Round-tripping your own encoder through your own decoder proves they agree with each other and nothing more. Every grammar and every message here is diffed against the EXI reference implementation, as documents and as signature fragments.

04

A decoder you can point at the internet

The EXI parser is the first thing to touch unauthenticated bytes from a cable a stranger plugged in. Every length is bounded by its schema facet before anything is allocated, nesting is capped, and the layers above hold the same line.

Protocol, not policy

The engines own framing, decoding, the handshake, session-id checking, message ordering and the spec timers. They own nothing about charging: whether to authorize a vehicle, which schedule to offer, how much current to deliver. Those arrive as events and are answered with a message you build.

  • One engine shape everywhere. handle_input, poll_event, respond, take_transmit, poll_timeout — the same five calls for the charger, the vehicle, discovery and SLAC.
  • The ordering rules are data. One graph per protocol generation, shared by both roles, so the two sides cannot disagree about what is legal next.
  • Including the rules about failing. A FAILED_* response ends the session and leaves only SessionStopReq; the drivers enter that state on their own.
  • And about stopping. Pause is not terminate, and renegotiating a schedule is neither.

What is implemented

Everything from the powerline link coming up to the charge loop turning.

And what is not

Named rather than implied. A library that is vague about its edges is one you find the edges of in the field.

V2G PKI

X.509 chain validation and the certificate-installation flows are not written. The message types are generated and signatures work; path validation and the key-agreement envelopes are the gap, and it is the one worth naming loudest.

Transport bindings

No TCP, no TLS, no raw sockets, by design. The examples show the twenty lines of glue for each, so you bring rustls on a server and embedded-tls on an ECU without this crate choosing.

DIN SPEC 70121

Its schemas are behind a paywall. Hand-transcribing them would produce a codec with no reference to check against — precisely the failure mode this project exists to avoid.
